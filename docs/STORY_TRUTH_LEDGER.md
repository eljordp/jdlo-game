# JDLO Story and Drama Ledger

The game is a life documentary told like a 2000s Nintendo adventure. It should
protect the emotional truth and the causal spine without pretending every
scene is a transcript. Compression, composites, reordered periods, heightened
dialogue, and cinematic set pieces are allowed when they make the story clearer.

## Non-negotiable opening

The intro is exactly:

`Based on a true story.`

Do not add an explanation, disclaimer, or second line.

## Working standard

- JP's current-session direction outranks old notes and old game dialogue.
- Existing dialogue is a draft, not proof.
- Exact money, dates, ages, titles, performance claims, client quotes, and
  résumé outcomes need a real basis. If the number is not important, omit it.
- A dramatic scene may combine people or moments, but it must not falsely turn
  a demo, conversation, or ghost into a paid client.
- Keep identities and mechanics mysterious where detail would expose private
  people or turn the story into an explanation dump.
- Explain the cause when the audience would otherwise misunderstand JP's
  decision or transformation.

## Locked direction from JP

- Friends call him JP or Jordi. Mom may use Jordan. The court may use Jordan
  Lopez.
- Ivy is a tan female French bulldog.
- Home is North Bay, not one flattened city: Fairfield, Suisun, and Napa are
  all part of the opening geography.
- Santa Barbara should be unmistakably Santa Barbara: coast, Spanish-revival
  materials, palms, social motion, beach life, and a lived-in house.
- LUNA was JP's first time trading. He put his full portfolio into it and it
  went effectively to zero. That loss helps cause the move into selling weed.
- The dramatic chapter order is intentionally weed rise → jail → Caymus. It is
  selected for impact, even if life periods are compressed or reordered.
- Build a real weed-rise chapter. The loop is supply, calls, weighing,
  deliveries, money, comfort, and rising paranoia—not one isolated drop.
- The legal charge was attempted murder, not intent to distribute. JP says he
  never intended or attempted to hurt anyone. Court owns the legal facts; the
  weed chapter should not invent a false one-to-one explanation of the charge.
- JP faced thirteen years and accepted a plea for one year. He had a lawyer,
  not a public defender.
- Jail unfolds as phases across the sentence, not three literal days: survival
  and fighting, discipline and training, then books/classes/faith. Mikey,
  Chris, and Bird provide continuity. Faith is part of the changed routine.
- Named jail material includes *The Compound Effect*, *The Intelligent
  Investor*, psychology coursework, anger management, and JP's return to faith.
- The tractor callback appears only at Caymus.
- Caymus is the post-jail dramatic chapter. It carries physical work, Spanish,
  vineyard routine, machinery, restlessness, and the arrival of ChatGPT/AI.
- JP is self-taught. He did not go to school for this work. The game should show
  tutorials, AI, failed builds, repetition, and delivery instead of having
  every NPC announce that he is gifted.
- Sticker Smith is the first legitimate client/payment at roughly $1K.
- WCT e-commerce, DHL Translator, and Vacaville Appliance are real delivered
  work. Other prospects, demos, and outreach must remain prospects, demos, and
  outreach unless JP confirms otherwise.
- Some prospects ask for a proposal, promise a call, read the message, keep
  watching social stories, and still ghost. That frustration belongs in the
  Come Up.
- DHL should feel serious and operational: a real warehouse, dock activity,
  multilingual workers, motion, and a tool grounded in the floor workflow.
- Vegas was real and should be a full lived event: Strip arrival, supercars,
  dayclub and pool, Marquee, Omnia, strip club, penthouse owners, business
  owners, women/dancers/partygoers, crowds in motion, food, drinking, smoking,
  and deals or introductions happening throughout the chaos.
- Vegas deals should be shown as meetings, introductions, follow-ups, and work
  moving forward—not an invented signed contract victory screen.

## Required versus optional

Required actions are only the actions that cause the next story state. Examples:
the LUNA loss, accepting the first weed run, completing the weed loop, jail's
fight/training/book/faith phases, the Caymus turning point, and the first real
client payment.

Food, drinks, smoking, drugs, concerts, beach games, driving, speeding, side
conversations, hidden rooms, memories, and joke interactions are optional
Easter eggs unless one directly causes the chapter transition.

## Tone control

JP is allowed to be unusually driven and characters are allowed to call him
different. Use it sparingly. The stronger proof is playable behavior:

- he keeps sending after being ghosted;
- he learns without a school structure;
- he delivers work that has real users;
- he survives consequences without turning jail into a superhero montage;
- he still has unfinished work, inconsistent outcomes, and things to learn.

The story should feel personal and emotional enough for people with similar
experiences to relate, and concrete enough for people without those experiences
to understand. Keep some names, exact mechanics, private motives, and future
outcomes mysterious.

## Map and scene rule

Every room must either be tight or earn its size. Large interiors need zones,
personal objects, material changes, motion, and believable furniture clusters.
No empty beige boxes. No decorative wall that looks passable but blocks the
player. Every required route must be walked in the rendered game before it is
rated.

## Ratings rule

Do not carry forward draft scores. Re-rate a section only after an end-to-end
playthrough verifies navigation, required actions, dialogue flow, minigames,
visual density, and transition to the next chapter.
